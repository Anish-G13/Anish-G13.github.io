01Executive overview

Bring the research and the decision into one workflow.

I designed, developed, and launched Vantage AI to help product managers, founders, consultants, and analysts compare products, review the evidence, and decide which gaps deserve attention.

The original product made feature comparison faster. Its next iteration addressed a more important question: could someone inspect, correct, and defend the resulting recommendation? V2 connects cited research, human judgment, weighted comparisons, and explicit roadmap assumptions in a publicly deployed product.

02Problem identification

The work is fragmented. The judgment still belongs to the user.

Competitive research often moves from vendor pages to spreadsheets to a presentation. Information must be gathered, compared on consistent criteria, checked for evidence, and translated into priorities. Each handoff adds work and makes the reasoning harder to trace.

A / Gather

Scattered information

Product documentation, feature pages, and pricing describe different scopes and plans.

B / Compare

Unclear confidence

A complete-looking AI matrix can hide unsupported claims and ambiguous evidence.

C / Decide

Gaps without priorities

A competitor having a feature does not establish customer demand or justify building it.

The product needed to make the reasoning inspectable, the assessments correctable, and the prioritization explicit.

This problem framing came from the initial workflow and a review of the product’s limitations. It is a product hypothesis, not a finding from customer interviews.

03Discovery & strategy

Start with a narrow decision, then build the complete path to it.

Core hypothesis: connecting research, evidence review, correction, and prioritization will reduce the work needed to produce a defensible competitive comparison.

Intended user personas and jobs to be done — hypotheses to test with external users.

Product managers
Compare alternatives and explain which capability gaps warrant discovery.
Founders
Assess positioning and choose where a limited product team should focus.
Consultants
Turn a reviewable comparison into a concise client decision brief.
Analysts
Inspect sources, correct assessments, and export a consistent benchmark.

What I prioritized

  1. Evidence and state correctness. Establish traceability and prevent stale findings.
  2. Human control. Let reviewers correct the matrix without losing their work.
  3. Decision support. Connect comparisons to transparent priorities and reusable exports.

How success should be measured

  • Time to an evidence-reviewed comparison, against a matched manual task.
  • Independent citation-support precision and uncertainty comprehension.
  • Unaided task completion, correction success, and perceived usefulness.
  • Consistency between the dashboard, priorities, and exported figures.

These are evaluation criteria. External user results are still to be measured.

04Development & execution

A connected workflow, from question to decision brief.

  1. 01Research

    Identify five criteria and research competitors.

  2. 02Review

    Inspect evidence and correct assessments.

  3. 03Compare

    Adjust importance and evaluate coverage.

  4. 04Prioritize

    Estimate impact and implementation effort.

  5. 05Export

    Take the comparison and roadmap into a brief.

01 / Research with provenance

Make each assessment inspectable.

Gemini identifies five comparison criteria. Google Search grounding can attach sources to individual assessments, with explanations, dates, and qualitative confidence. Unsupported claims remain Unverified. Reviewers can edit ratings and add notes while retaining the original research.

YesPartialNoUnverified
02 / Compare without hiding uncertainty

Show the score and how much is known.

Importance weights drive the competitive scores and bar chart. The radar chart shows feature profiles. Unverified ratings are excluded from the known-feature score, while evidence coverage and bounds show what remains uncertain. Manual corrections recalculate the comparison immediately.

03 / Prioritize with visible assumptions

Turn a difference into a next action.

Users adjust impact and effort to rank opportunities. Each item includes the competitive gap, rationale, and recommended next action. Missing evidence becomes a research task. Comparison CSV, roadmap CSV, and executive PDF exports use the same current calculations as the dashboard.

Priority = competitive gap × importance × impact ÷ effort

Gap compares your rating with the strongest assessed competitor. Importance is weighted 1–10; impact and effort are relative 1–5 assumptions. These are not measured customer value or business returns.

05V1 → V2

Keep the useful comparison. Strengthen the decision behind it.

The initial application proved out the comparison workflow. The V2 scope addressed gaps visible in the implementation: unsupported ratings, limited user correction, a simple gap list, and fragile result handling.

06Product decisions

Three choices that shaped the product.

Open each decision to explore the problem, alternatives, rationale, and trade-off.

Decision 01Evidence quality over apparent completeness
Problem
A complete AI-generated matrix can appear more certain than the underlying evidence.
Alternatives
Fill every cell from model knowledge; attach broad page-level citations; or require sources linked to the individual claim.
Decision
Accept supported assessments and classify insufficiently supported claims as Unverified.
Rationale
A reviewer should be able to inspect the basis for a claim. Missing evidence is not proof that a capability is absent.
Trade-off
The matrix may be less complete, and reviewers still need to check source quality, product identity, and plan scope.
Decision 02Human control over automated certainty
Problem
AI assessments can be incomplete or wrong, and a fixed answer makes correction difficult.
Alternatives
Require repeated AI requests; make the generated matrix read-only; or let users correct it directly.
Decision
Add editable ratings and optional notes, preserve the originals, and recalculate every dependent result.
Rationale
The user owns the decision. Corrections should survive weight changes and remain auditable.
Trade-off
Manual changes are labeled judgments, not independently verified facts. Session work must be exported before leaving.
Decision 03Practical prioritization over unnecessary complexity
Problem
A gap list offers limited guidance, but an elaborate scoring model would imply business evidence the product does not yet have.
Alternatives
Keep an unranked list; introduce reach and revenue estimates; or use a transparent impact/effort framework.
Decision
Rank assessed gaps using editable importance, impact, and effort assumptions. Keep the product on Streamlit.
Rationale
Users can understand and challenge the calculation while the existing Python stack supports the complete workflow.
Trade-off
The score is directional, not validated ROI. Persistent accounts, shared workspaces, and extra infrastructure remain deferred until demand is established.

What is working

What remains to be established

External user value, independent citation accuracy, repeat usage, and business outcomes. A functional release and engineering tests do not establish customer adoption or product-market fit.
