01Investment overview

H&R Block provides assisted and do-it-yourself tax preparation services. The acquisition thesis pairs recurring tax-compliance demand and a low-capital-expenditure business model with the opportunity to improve client retention, digital economics, and operating efficiency.

The central underwriting question is whether that cash generation can support a leveraged acquisition at the assumed price. The model tests a five-year hold from after FY2026 through FY2031, including the cost of acquisition financing and a cash sweep toward debt reduction.

02LBO transaction structure

A 25% premium to the case’s $50.08 reference share price produces a $62.60 assumed offer, $7.72bn of purchase equity value, and $8.25bn of entry enterprise value. Entry valuation is 7.81× FY2026 EBITDA.

Opening financing combines a 3.0× EBITDA first-lien term loan at 8.5% cash interest with 1.5× EBITDA senior notes at 9.5%. Total opening debt is 4.5× EBITDA; the sponsor contributes $4.21bn, or 42.4% of total uses.

03Financial modeling

The base operating plan begins with the supplied FY2027 company-guidance midpoints for revenue and adjusted EBITDA. Revenue then grows 4% annually, while the EBITDA margin increases to 28.2% by FY2031. Capital expenditure is 2.1% of sales; incremental working capital is 1.5% of the annual revenue increase; normalized cash taxes use a 23% rate.

05Investment thesis

Value creation through operations and cash

  • Retention and cross-sell: Improve assisted-client repeat rates and expand advisory offerings, subject to evidence on customer cohorts, attach rates, and revenue per return.
  • Digital unit economics: Improve conversion and retention in paid DIY, automated workflows, and Wave subscriptions while protecting contribution margins.
  • Cash discipline: Reduce unproductive store and overhead spending without weakening service, cybersecurity, or seasonal capacity; direct available cash toward debt reduction.

Risks to the acquisition case

  • Demand and pricing: DIY competition, automation, free-filing alternatives, and price sensitivity could erode volumes or margins.
  • Seasonality and credit: Annual closing cash does not establish liquidity through tax season. Revolver capacity, covenants, financing costs, and interest deductibility need underwriting.
  • Exit and execution: The assumed multiple, productivity improvements, cash availability, and debt sweep may not be achievable. Integration and restructuring costs could absorb cash.

06Investment Committee decision

Pass at the 25% modeled offer premium. The base-case gross IRR of 16.7% falls 3.3 percentage points below the 20% hurdle. A 1.8% downside IRR offers limited compensation for the financial and operating risks of a leveraged acquisition.

The 26.6% upside case demonstrates potential under stronger assumptions; it does not resolve the shortfall in the base underwriting. Revisit the acquisition only at a lower effective entry price, with verifiable incremental cash generation, and after seasonal liquidity and credit capacity are fully underwritten.

Source basis & items requiring review

This page summarizes the supplied October 2026 model, committee memo, and presentation. Historical company figures and market reference inputs are reproduced from those materials; hypothetical acquisition terms, forecasts, financing, and returns are identified separately.

The original memo and presentation describe an approximate 16.5% acquisition premium as meeting the hurdle. Recalculation of the workbook gives an approximately 16.23% break-even premium; 16.5% produces a gross IRR slightly below 20%. The original files are retained unchanged.

Cash accessibility, seasonal revolver needs, fully diluted share count, lender terms, and tax deductibility require independent diligence. The annual model omits an intrayear revolver, some purchase-accounting effects, lease debt capitalization, and detailed tax and working-capital timing. Modeled gross returns are hypothetical investment-case outputs, not realized performance.
