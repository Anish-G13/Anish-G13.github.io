01Overview

H&R Block offers assisted and do-it-yourself tax preparation. Recurring demand and low capex support the buyout thesis, with potential to improve retention, digital profitability, and operating efficiency.

I tested whether cash generation supports the assumed price over a five-year hold after FY2026 through FY2031, including financing costs and cash sweeps to repay debt.

02Transaction structure

A 25% premium to the $50.08 reference price implies a $62.60 assumed offer: $7.72bn purchase equity value and $8.25bn enterprise value, or 7.81× FY2026 EBITDA.

Assumed financing: a 3.0× EBITDA first-lien term loan at 8.5% cash interest and 1.5× EBITDA senior notes at 9.5%. Opening debt is 4.5× EBITDA; sponsor equity is $4.21bn, or 42.4% of uses.

03Financial model

The base forecast starts at FY2027 revenue and adjusted EBITDA guidance midpoints. Assumptions: 4% annual revenue growth, 28.2% EBITDA margin by FY2031, capex at 2.1% of sales, incremental working capital at 1.5% of the annual revenue increase, and 23% normalized cash taxes.

05Investment thesis

Operating improvements

  • Retention and cross-sell: Improve assisted-client retention and advisory sales, subject to cohort, attach-rate, and revenue-per-return evidence.
  • Digital unit economics: Improve paid DIY conversion, retention, automation, and Wave subscriptions while protecting contribution margins.
  • Cash discipline: Cut unproductive store and overhead costs while preserving service, cybersecurity, and seasonal capacity; use available cash to repay debt.

Key risks

  • Demand and pricing: DIY competition, automation, free filing, and price sensitivity could erode volumes or margins.
  • Seasonality and credit: Year-end cash does not establish tax-season liquidity. Revolver capacity, covenants, financing costs, and interest deductibility need review.
  • Exit and execution: Exit multiples, productivity gains, cash availability, and debt sweeps may disappoint. Integration and restructuring could consume cash.

06Investment decision

Pass at the 25% modeled offer premium. Base-case gross IRR of 16.7% is 3.3 percentage points below the 20% hurdle. The 1.8% downside IRR leaves little compensation for buyout risks.

The 26.6% upside depends on stronger assumptions. I would reconsider at a lower entry price, with verified cash-flow improvements and sufficient seasonal liquidity and borrowing capacity.

Sources and model limitations

Company figures and reference prices come from the October 2026 model, memo, and presentation. Acquisition terms, forecasts, financing, and returns are hypothetical.

The memo, presentation, and workbook use a maximum premium of about 16.23%, or $58.21 per share, to meet the modeled 20% gross IRR hurdle while holding base operations and financing fixed.

Further diligence is needed on cash accessibility, seasonal borrowing, fully diluted shares, lender terms, and tax deductibility. The annual model omits an intrayear revolver, some purchase accounting, lease debt capitalization, and detailed tax and working-capital timing. Gross returns are modeled, not realized.
