01Project overview

The project combines financial-statement research, an operating forecast through FY2030, an unlevered discounted cash-flow valuation, and a public-market peer comparison. Bear, base, and bull cases test the effect of growth, profitability, discount rates, and illustrative acquisition outcomes.

The analysis separates reported results from analyst assumptions. It also distinguishes company-reported free cash flow from the unlevered cash flow used in the DCF, and treats the proposed Delivery Hero acquisition as a pending event rather than a completed transaction.

02Investment thesis

Mobility and Delivery economics

Greater trip volume, delivery density, and consumer engagement could support operating leverage and cash generation. The model links gross-bookings growth, reported revenue presentation, and GAAP operating margins rather than treating revenue growth alone as a complete measure of demand.

UK Mobility business-model changes affect recognized revenue, so revenue divided by bookings is not treated as a pure economic take rate. The research also flags changes in segment-profit definitions that limit direct comparisons across periods.

Autonomous vehicles: opportunity and risk

Uber’s demand network and partnerships could help aggregate autonomous supply without owning every vehicle. The opposing case is that vertically integrated robotaxi fleets bypass the marketplace, or that autonomy requires substantially more capital than the standalone forecast assumes.

Growth catalysts and thesis checkpoints

Sustained bookings growth, durable margin gains, and capital-efficient AV monetization would strengthen the case. The October research identifies Q3 earnings on 4 November 2026 and the announced Delivery Hero offer deadline on 5 November as checkpoints for operating performance, financing, and deal risk.

03DCF & scenarios

The DCF projects after-tax operating profit, adds depreciation and amortization, and deducts capital expenditure and incremental working capital. It bridges enterprise value to equity through debt, unrestricted cash, financial investments, and a 2.5% share-dilution cushion.

The base case uses 10.0% WACC and 2.75% terminal growth. Approximately 78.5% of standalone enterprise value comes from terminal value, making the result particularly sensitive to long-run assumptions.

05Risks & decision

  • Economics: Insurance costs, incentives, regulation, and weaker unit returns could prevent margin expansion even if trips grow.
  • Capital allocation: Acquisition financing and autonomous-fleet investment could increase leverage and reduce cash returns. The research does not assume a completed acquisition or verified merger synergies.
  • Valuation: Discount rates, terminal growth, investment carrying values, and dilution can materially change modeled value.

The Watchlist / Neutral stance reflects limited margin of safety under the base assumptions. A better entry price, evidence of durable profit growth, and clearer acquisition and AV economics would warrant renewed underwriting.
