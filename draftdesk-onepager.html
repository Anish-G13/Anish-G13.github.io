DraftDesk

Context-aware QBRs and escalation summaries with source-linked facts and human review

Anish GudipaatiRead the full PRD ↓

01Problem & opportunity

Problem and initial user

CS managers repeatedly turn account data, call notes, and internal knowledge into QBRs and escalation summaries. Gathering and checking context can take as much effort as writing.

Start with a CS manager and a team-lead buyer. The original persona manages 40 accounts and produces 8–12 account documents monthly; the product must fit that actual cadence.

Competitive position

Microsoft already offers CRM-aware account summaries and meeting preparation, including Salesforce support. Notion added Salesforce search in March 2026. [D1–D3]

DraftDesk must prove lower end-to-end preparation and review time for a specific CS document. An integration pipeline alone is insufficient differentiation.

02Product approach

Product decision

Start with two related outputs and a complete review loop: choose account and template → collect permitted evidence → confirm context → generate → verify and edit → export.

QBRs need supported progress and next actions. Escalations need reliable chronology and commitments. RFP responses and marketing briefs follow after the first source and quality contracts work.

03Requirements

Context and permissions

Gong OAuth authorization is organization-level and requires a tech admin. Integration access does not automatically authorize every DraftDesk user to view every call. [D4, D5]

Map each customer’s Salesforce fields explicitly and preserve record, object, and field permissions. Missing or inaccessible context stays visible; the model must not fill it with plausible facts. [D6]

Acceptance criteria

Revenue, renewal dates, customer outcomes, and commitments link to evidence or explicit user confirmation. Conflicting sources require a decision. Regeneration preserves accepted edits through version history.

Export uses the reviewed version and preserves document structure. A downloaded draft is not counted as sent to a customer.

04Validation

AI quality evaluation

Proposed release suite: 40 approved or simulated account packets, split between QBRs and escalations. Include stale data, contradictions, missing fields, wrong-account matches, and malicious instructions inside transcripts.

Gates: 100% fidelity for critical numbers and dates in the suite; ≥95% supported claims; zero critical unsupported claims or unauthorized disclosures.

Pilot and success metrics

Run a proposed five-team pilot against each team’s actual current workflow. Compare matched tasks and blind-review output quality.

Primary gate: ≥30% less total human preparation and review time at equal or better quality. Retain ≥70% acceptance with fewer than two substantive revision rounds and 3+ weekly drafts as targets, segmented by document cadence.

Generation latency target: p95 ≤60 seconds; it is not a measured result.

05Economics

Pricing and cost discipline

Proposed Free: 5 drafts/month. Pro: $18/seat/month. Team: $14/seat/month for 5+ seats.

7,500 seats yield $1.62M annualized at all-Pro pricing, $1.26M at all-Team pricing, or $1.44M at a 50/50 mix.

At $14 and a 75% contribution margin, variable cost must stay ≤$3.50 per seat/month. Validate generation, connector, and support costs before promising unlimited usage.

06Next decisions

13-week sequence

Weeks 1–2: observe work, qualify partners, define permissions and evaluation.
Weeks 3–5: two templates, manual context, evidence review, and versioned editing.
Weeks 6–8: approved connectors and reliable DOCX export.
Weeks 9–11: pilot and paired workflow measurement.
Weeks 12–13: hardening and paid-continuation decisions.

Expansion is gated on quality and buyer evidence.

What would change the decision

Stop affected retrieval after a permission failure. Hold expansion if fact-checking erases the writing-time savings. Reconsider the wedge if the team’s existing Microsoft, Notion, or Gong workflow performs equally well.

The next investment case rests on an evaluation report, observed time savings, a tested permissions design, and paid-pilot decisions—not an assumed launch ranking.
